Six rules for the room

Earned in Tuesday's hour · they apply to the 19th too
What worked, and keep doing

You put the two gating questions in writing before the meeting and got both answered before you paid for strategy. You made the hardest disclosure yourself, unforced. You owned the adverse side of the ledger without hedging. And you asked one question that produced the best analysis of the week.

What cost you

Every damaging moment in the hour arrived in a pause that you closed. The answer was already complete; the silence after it was not yours to fill. Detail sits in the private file — the pattern is the point here, and the pattern is executional, which means it is fixable in two weeks.

  • Answer, then stop. One sentence. If they want more they'll ask. The clause after the comma is where cases are lost.
  • Never characterise your own intent. “I was worried about rent” is a fact. “I never meant it as a threat” is an argument, and it invites the follow-up that hurts.
  • Never say their word for them. Don't utter extortion, threat, immigration, or fraud. Make them say it.
  • Silence is not yours to fix. Count to three.
  • Ask before you explain. Lead with the question; add context only if it's wanted.
  • Budget for the moot. It is the line item that matters most for you specifically. Don't let it be the one cut for cost.
