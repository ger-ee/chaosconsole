Standing alerts

Things the calendar already decided

Verified against LASC & statute · 03 Aug 2026
Correction
The judge is Angela J. Davis, not David W. Swift
The directory naming Judge Swift in Dept 547 is dated 2022 and is stale — Swift now sits at Santa Monica. Two current LASC sources (departments directory rev. 5/5/26, judicial directory w/c 7/27/26) both place Judge Angela J. Davis in 547. Clerk line (213) 633-5859. Do not walk into a consultation naming the wrong bench officer.
Trap
Filing the written response may cost you the free continuance
Family Code §245(a) gives a respondent one continuance as of right — worded as being "to respond to the petition." Practitioner guidance reads that right as spent once you file a response. The written response is itself optional and has no deadline. So the order of operations is a real decision with real cost. Put this question to the first attorney who picks up, before anything gets filed.
Closed
Discovery is barred by default in these cases since 2024
Family Code §6309 (SB 741) shuts off the Civil Discovery Act in restraining-order matters except on a good-cause showing to the judge. You cannot simply issue subpoenas to a bank, a carrier, or a building manager and expect them to bite. Practical consequence: gather the records you are personally entitled to yourself — your own statements, your own logs, your own account exports — which needs no subpoena and no leave of court.
Tomorrow
Aug 4 is the last mail date for a consumer-records notice
A records subpoena returnable on the 19th needs its CCP §1985.3 Notice to Consumer served 10 days out, +5 for mail. Mailed after tomorrow, the arithmetic fails. The business-records deposition route (SUBP-020, 20 days from issuance) is already foreclosed for this hearing date.
Wed Aug 5
Remote-appearance notice deadline, if any witness needs one
A contested hearing is an evidentiary hearing, so CRC 3.672 wants notice 10 court days out — Wednesday. For yourself, appear in person regardless: these hearings turn on credibility and remote is a discretionary ask you do not need to make.
Required
A witness list is the one thing genuinely mandatory before the hearing
Family Code §217(c) requires a witness list with a brief description of anticipated testimony, filed and served before the hearing, for any non-party witness. Form FL-321. Without it the judge can demand an offer of proof before letting anyone testify. No filing fee applies — §6222 exempts these filings.

How this list was built, and what it is worth
Every credential below was checked against the State Bar's own licensee and certified-specialist registry, not against firm marketing — and the registry contradicts the marketing more often than not. Directory badges (Super Lawyers, Avvo, Lawyers of Distinction) are advertising placements and carry no quality signal; they are ignored here. Nobody on this list has been contacted, no fee is confirmed, and none of this is a recommendation. Two facts do most of the sorting: who is actually certified, and who actually defends respondents in family court rather than defendants in criminal court. Those are different jobs in different buildings.

What you actually say

The scripts

Read it aloud once before the first call

On the phone

Ninety seconds · you are booking a slot, not consulting
Open — flat, unhurried

"Good morning. My name is [name]. I'm the respondent in a contested domestic-violence restraining order matter in family court, and I need a consultation today or tomorrow."

The three facts that get you triaged correctly

"The hearing is Wednesday the nineteenth of August, eight thirty, Department 547 at Stanley Mosk. I was personally served on the thirtieth of July. I'm defending, not filing."

Why you are worth a slot

"The case is documentary rather than testimonial — the central evidence is a very large message archive between the parties, which is already indexed and analysed. I have a prepared exhibit-ready analysis I can bring with me."

"I'm prepared to retain quickly, and I'm looking for a flat fee quoted by phase with a written installment schedule rather than an hourly retainer."

Ask, then stop talking

"Can you tell me your earliest availability, and the name of the attorney I'd be seeing?"

Before you hang up — every time

"Two quick things. Is the consultation free or is there a fee? And can I get that appointment confirmed by email?"

  • Do not consult the screener. Intake staff are not lawyers and the conversation is not protected the way you need it to be. Book the slot; save the facts for the attorney.
  • Do not describe the allegations on an intake call. "Contested restraining order, respondent, hearing on the nineteenth" is the whole of it.
  • Do not put anything sensitive in a web contact form. At least one of these firms warns on its own site that its form is unencrypted. Assume the rest are too.
  • Do not accept a verbal fee. Written, by phase, or it did not happen.

In the room

Forty minutes · the difficult part comes from you, first
Frame it in the first thirty seconds

"Before anything else — I want to walk you through the parts of this that are bad for me, because I'd rather you hear them from me now than find them in the file later. Then I'd like to ask you the same set of questions I'm asking everyone."

Then do exactly that

Give them the unfavourable material plainly and without softening it. An attorney who hears the worst of it up front and stays engaged is worth more than one who is impressed by the favourable half. Their reaction in that first ten minutes is the most useful data you will collect all week.

Watch for the ones who disqualify themselves

Any guarantee of outcome ends the interview. So does refusing to name who appears on the nineteenth, hourly-only billing with no cap, no visible reaction to the criminal dimension, or eagerness to start filing things immediately.

Close, every time

"Can you put the fee in writing before I leave — quoted by phase, with the installment schedule and the name of the attorney appearing on the nineteenth?"

  • Same ten questions, same order, every firm. The comparability is the whole point of doing three of these.
  • Score each one in the car, before the next. Impressions decay within the hour and blur into each other by evening.
  • Bring the documents and let them read. Describing an archive is worthless; showing one is not.
  • Ask what they think the fight is really about. The good answer is specific and slightly uncomfortable. The bad answer is a brochure.
The scorecard

Ten questions, unchanged

Ask all ten · score all ten · compare
01
How many contested hearings of this kind have you tried to decision in the last 24 months — and how many in this district?
Wrong answer: a number about the practice area generally. You want hearings, to decision, here.
02
Who personally appears on the nineteenth? Will that name go in the fee agreement?
Refusal to name it, or to write it down, is disqualifying.
03
Will you quote a flat fee for this phase — and what exactly does it include? A continuance, a second session, a moot?
"It depends" is an answer about them, not about the case.
04
Will you take a down payment with the balance on a written installment schedule — and what happens if one is late?
An evergreen retainer is not an installment plan. An empty trust account mid-case invites a motion to withdraw at the worst moment.
05
The petition quotes selectively from a very long record. Walk me through how you force in the surrounding context.
You are listening for completeness doctrine, unprompted. This is the technical question that separates them.
06
Discovery is largely shut off in these cases since 2024. How do you get documents in front of the court by the nineteenth?
If they do not know §6309 exists, that is the end of it. The good answer involves records you already hold.
07
Would you take the continuance here, and why? Does filing a response first cost me the automatic one?
The single most consequential procedural question on the board. Ask it first if you only get one.
08
How would you coordinate with separate criminal counsel — and who decides what I say under oath?
The right answer names someone other than themselves as holding that veto.
09
Having skimmed this, what outcome bands do you see?
Any guarantee, in either direction, ends the interview on the spot.
10
What is the written worst-case total through the hearing?
Ask for it as a number on paper. Watch what happens to their face.
